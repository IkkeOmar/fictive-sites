Why this chair

Mesh back. Because lumbar support requires airflow. A foam-and-fabric backrest traps heat against the spine; over an 8-hour day, the body notices. The Officraft mesh is 60% recycled polyester, with an elastomer frame that holds its shape for the 12-year warranty period and beyond. The mesh tension is calibrated to give 18 mm of give at the lumbar peak — enough to follow the spine's natural curve, not enough to feel like a hammock.

4D armrests. Because the shoulder is a 3-dimensional joint, and a 2D armrest is a compromise. The OF-CHR-001 armrests adjust in four directions: height, width, depth, and pivot. The pivot is the one most chairs skip. It matters when you type, because the forearm wants to rotate inward as the shoulder extends. A flat armrest blocks that rotation; a pivoting one doesn't.

12-year warranty. Because we expect this chair to last 12 years. The frame is 65% recycled aluminum, the base is 88% recycled die-cast aluminum, the gas lift is Class 4 (100,000 cycles — the highest commercial grade). At 8 hours of use per day, 5 days a week, 48 weeks a year, that's 19,200 hours. The warranty covers the structural components for 12 years. The mechanisms — gas lift, tilt mechanism, castors — are 5 years. We hold spare parts for 10 years after discontinuation, which is 4 years longer than the warranty. We do not want to sell you a chair and then make you landfill it because we discontinued a gas lift size.

Sustainability and ergonomics are not separate departments at Officraft. The chair you're looking at is the same chair a procurement manager and a sustainability lead can both sign off on, because the data is on the page, not in a separate PDF.
